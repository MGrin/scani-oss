## Summary

A holding's past balance is not stored. It is computed on demand by
the balance engine, `balanceAt(evidence, at)`, from two things the
holding records:

- its **readings**: balances a feed or a person reported at an
  instant ([observations](/concepts/observations/));
- its **ledger**: the [transactions](/concepts/transactions/) that
  moved it.

`BalanceAtTimeService.getBalance(holdingId, at)` is the entry point
for one [holding](/concepts/holdings/) at one instant. The same engine
writes today's `holdings.balance`, so the past and the present come
from one rule.

## Which readings count

Not every reading anchors a balance. Which ones do depends on the
holding's kind:

- **A feed holding** is one an exchange, wallet, broker or statement
  feeds. Its feed's checkpoints anchor it. A value a person types on
  it is kept as a *verification*: it is compared with the feed's
  figure and never anchors.
- **A snapshot holding** is one a person keeps. Their snapshots
  anchor it.

On a feed holding, a snapshot taken for a stretch no feed covers can
still anchor that stretch.

## How a balance is found

For a holding and an instant `at`:

1. **Before the holding starts, it is absent.** `balance` is null and
   `beforeRecords` is true. The start is the earliest of the holding's
   creation, its first kept reading and its first ledger entry.
2. **After a reading, walk forward.** Take the latest anchoring reading
   at or before `at` and add every ledger entry since it. This is the
   usual case (`anchor: 'observation-before'`).
3. **Before the first reading, walk back.** Take the first anchoring
   reading and subtract the entries between `at` and it
   (`anchor: 'observation-after'`). A snapshot holding holds its first
   value back to its start instead, so its balance cannot jump where
   its history begins.
4. **With no reading at all, sum the ledger** from the start
   (`anchor: null`).

```ts
interface BalanceAtTimeResult {
  balance: Decimal | null;
  anchor: 'observation-after' | 'observation-before' | null;
  anchorAt: Date | null;
  beforeRecords: boolean;
}
```

## No line and no floor

- **No line across a gap.** Between two readings only the ledger moves
  the balance. When the second reading disagrees with the first plus
  the ledger, the balance steps at the second reading. The money side
  books that step as unexplained drift on the same instant, so flows
  and the value history agree.
- **No floor.** A balance goes negative when the ledger says so,
  usually because an import starts partway through a holding's life.
  It is shown as computed rather than clamped to zero, so the gap stays
  visible and can be answered.

## How the rollup uses it

The nightly [portfolio-value rollup](/concepts/rollup/) calls
`balancesFor`. It loads each holding's readings and ledger once, 25
holdings at a time, and answers every day of the window from memory.
An ad-hoc caller, such as a chart request, calls `getBalance` and lets
the service load what it needs.

## What it doesn't do

- **It does not write.** The ledger, the readings and the stored
  balance are untouched.
- **It does not price.** The balance is in the token's units (BTC,
  AAPL, EUR, …); the rollup values it against the
  [price graph](/concepts/pricing/).

<Aside type="note" title="Why the engine replaced the old walk">
  Until October 2026 a past balance was walked from whichever balance
  seemed most trustworthy, including today's stored one, then clamped
  at zero and interpolated across gaps. Each of those choices could
  disagree with the ledger and with the other screens. The engine has
  one rule for every day, and the stored balance is its output, never
  its input.
</Aside>
